
# TikTok Content Production Manual

## 1. Purpose

This manual defines the practical workflow for turning a researched topic into a finished TikTok video.

It applies to:
- Travel Alerts.ke
- Simo Traveller

The two brands must retain separate audiences, voices, visual identities and content objectives.

## 2. Production Workflow

Every video should pass through these stages:

1. Topic selection
2. Research and verification
3. Audience and angle selection
4. Scriptwriting
5. Visual planning
6. Recording and asset collection
7. Editing
8. Quality control
9. Publishing
10. Performance tracking

Do not skip verification for factual or time-sensitive content.

## 3. Stage One: Topic Selection

Before production, establish:

- Which brand the video serves.
- The intended audience.
- The content pillar.
- Why the topic matters now.
- What viewers should learn or do.
- Whether the topic offers a distinct angle.

Reject topics that are irrelevant, repetitive, weakly sourced or unlikely to provide audience value.

## 4. Stage Two: Research and Verification

### Travel Alerts.ke

For changing travel information, prioritise:
1. Government and immigration authorities.
2. Official travel advisories.
3. Airports and airlines.
4. Aviation regulators and transport authorities.
5. WHO and relevant public-health authorities.
6. Reputable news organisations for additional context.

Record:
- What happened.
- Where it happened.
- When it happened.
- Publication date and event date, separately.
- Who is affected.
- Practical consequences for travellers.
- Required traveller action.
- Source links.
- Verification status.

Never present a rumour as a confirmed alert.

### Simo Traveller

For destination and travel guides, verify:
- Location and accessibility.
- Entry requirements.
- Opening hours and visitor restrictions.
- Fees and booking requirements.
- Transport options.
- Accommodation information.
- Seasonal considerations.
- Safety and responsible travel advice.

Clearly distinguish researched information from first-hand experience.

## 5. Stage Three: Choose the Video Angle

A topic is not yet a video idea.

Choose one clear audience benefit.

Examples:

Travel Alerts.ke:
- What changed?
- Who is affected?
- What should travellers do?
- What is the latest official guidance?

Simo Traveller:
- Why visit this destination?
- How can someone get there?
- What should visitors expect?
- What does a practical trip involve?

Avoid trying to explain too many unrelated things in one video.

## 6. Stage Four: Scriptwriting

Every script should have:

### Opening hook
Capture attention immediately with a relevant question, development or useful promise.

### Main information
Explain the most important information in a logical order.

### Context
Give viewers the background they need to understand the subject.

### Practical value
Explain what viewers can do, consider or prepare for.

### Closing
End with a relevant call to action, not an unrelated request for engagement.

Keep sentences natural and suitable for spoken delivery.

Do not use sensational claims that the evidence cannot support.

## 7. Stage Five: Visual Planning

Create a scene-by-scene plan before editing.

For every scene specify:
- Approximate duration.
- Visual or footage required.
- Voiceover line.
- On-screen text.
- Source or asset location.
- Transition where necessary.

Use footage that genuinely represents the subject.

Do not use unrelated visuals in a way that could mislead viewers.

### Travel Alerts.ke visual direction

- Deep navy for serious safety, border and advisory alerts.
- Tangerine/orange for travel developments and opportunities.
- White, bold and readable typography.
- Minimal graphics.
- Clear Travel Alerts.ke branding.
- No unnecessary flags, decorative icons or clutter.

### Simo Traveller visual direction

- Prioritise genuine destination scenery and relevant travel footage.
- Use natural, cinematic visual storytelling.
- Keep text readable and limited.
- Match footage to the narration.
- Avoid presenting stock footage as personal travel footage.

## 8. Stage Six: Recording and Asset Collection

Before recording:
- Confirm the final script.
- Check pronunciation of names and places.
- Gather relevant footage and images.
- Confirm permissions and usage rights.
- Prepare voiceover and editing assets.

Audio should be clear and understandable.

Use music only where it supports the content and is appropriately licensed or permitted.

## 9. Stage Seven: Editing

Editing should improve clarity and retention, not distract from the message.

Check:
- Strong opening.
- Appropriate pacing.
- Clear voiceover.
- Accurate subtitles.
- Relevant visuals.
- Consistent branding.
- No unnecessary pauses.
- Readable on-screen text.
- Appropriate video dimensions.

Use vertical 9:16 framing for standard TikTok videos.

Do not add effects merely for decoration.

## 10. Stage Eight: Quality Control

Before publishing, ask:

### Accuracy
- Are factual claims verified?
- Are dates and figures correct?
- Are sources reliable?
- Is uncertainty clearly identified?

### Audience value
- Is the purpose clear?
- Does the video answer a real question?
- Is the practical value easy to understand?

### Presentation
- Is the hook strong?
- Can viewers understand the audio?
- Are captions and subtitles readable?
- Do visuals match the subject?

### Brand integrity
- Is the correct brand identity used?
- Is the tone appropriate?
- Is there any misleading claim?

If an important fact is uncertain, correct the script or hold publication.

## 11. Stage Nine: Publishing Preparation

Prepare:
- Final video.
- Caption.
- Search-friendly keywords.
- Relevant hashtags.
- Call to action.
- Source references where appropriate.
- Publication date and time.

Use descriptive language that reflects the actual video.

Do not use irrelevant trending hashtags.

Human approval is required before publication unless explicit publishing authority has been granted.

## 12. Stage Ten: Performance Tracking

Record the published video in:

`analytics/performance-tracker.csv`

Capture available metrics at consistent intervals, preferably:
- 24 hours.
- 7 days.
- 30 days.

Track:
- Views.
- Average watch time.
- Completion rate.
- Shares.
- Saves.
- Comments.
- Profile views.
- Followers gained.
- Traffic sources where available.

Use `analytics/weekly-reports.md` to interpret performance.

Use `experiments/testing-log.md` for controlled tests.

## 13. Production Checklist

Before a video is marked ready:

- [ ] Correct brand selected.
- [ ] Topic has clear audience value.
- [ ] Research completed.
- [ ] Important claims verified.
- [ ] Video angle selected.
- [ ] Hook and script approved.
- [ ] Visuals planned and checked.
- [ ] Assets have appropriate usage rights.
- [ ] Editing completed.
- [ ] Captions and on-screen text checked.
- [ ] Quality control completed.
- [ ] Publishing details prepared.
- [ ] Human approval obtained.
- [ ] Performance tracking prepared.

## 14. Final Production Principle

A finished video is not simply an edited clip.

It is a complete piece of communication that combines accurate information, a clear audience benefit, strong storytelling and appropriate presentation.

Prioritise trust, usefulness and continuous improvement over unnecessary production complexity.
