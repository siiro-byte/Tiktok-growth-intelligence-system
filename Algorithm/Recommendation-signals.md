
# TikTok Recommendation Signals

## Purpose
This document records what is publicly known about TikTok's recommendation systems and translates it into practical, testable guidance for Travel Alerts.ke and Simo Traveller.

It is not a claim to know TikTok's private ranking formula. TikTok says its systems use multiple factors, and their relative importance can change.

## 1. The Three Broad Factor Groups

### A. User Interactions
Examples include:
- Videos a person likes, shares, comments on or watches in full.
- Videos a person skips or indicates they are not interested in.
- Accounts followed and content created.
- Search and other in-app activity.

Practical implication: Make each video useful to a clearly defined audience. Earn attention and meaningful interaction rather than asking for meaningless engagement.

### B. Content Information
TikTok identifies information such as:
- Captions
- Sounds
- Hashtags

Practical implication:
- Make the subject clear in spoken words.
- Use relevant on-screen text.
- Write descriptive captions.
- Use accurate, relevant hashtags.
- Avoid unrelated trending hashtags.

### C. User and Device Information
Examples include:
- Language preference
- Country or location-related settings
- Device type and account settings

TikTok has said these signals generally carry less weight than stronger signals of expressed interest.

Practical implication: Use language your intended audience understands. Do not assume that being based in Kenya limits your content to Kenyan viewers.

## 2. Ranking Is Personalised

TikTok selects eligible content and ranks it according to predictions about what an individual viewer may find relevant or interesting.

This means:
- There is no single universal For You feed.
- Two people can receive different recommendations.
- Similar-interest audiences can influence discovery.
- Videos can reach people who do not follow the creator.
- Recommendation outcomes are not guaranteed.

## 3. Watch Behaviour Matters

TikTok identifies watching a longer video from beginning to end as a strong signal of interest.

Monitor:
- Average watch time
- Average percentage watched, if available
- Completion rate, if available
- Retention, if available
- Rewatches, where reported
- Shares
- Saves
- Comments
- Follows generated

Do not treat one metric as a complete explanation. Compare videos of similar length and topic.

## 4. Follower Count Is Not a Guarantee

TikTok's published explanation says follower count and previous high-performing videos are not direct recommendation factors.

A larger follower base can still produce more initial views because more people may see a post through their Following feed.

Practical implication: Small accounts can still earn discovery. Focus on individual video quality and relevance.

## 5. Eligibility and Safety Matter

Not every video that remains on TikTok is necessarily eligible for For You recommendation.

For Travel Alerts.ke:
- Verify claims before publishing.
- Attribute important information to official sources.
- Avoid sensational or misleading headlines.
- Distinguish confirmed restrictions from enhanced screening, advisories or proposals.
- Correct outdated information promptly.

For Simo Traveller:
- Avoid misleading destination and safety claims.
- Respect filming restrictions and local rules.
- Never present stock footage as personal experience.
- Use licensed or original footage and audio.

## 6. Practical Application by Brand

### Travel Alerts.ke

Build videos around one clear traveller question:
- What changed?
- Who is affected?
- When does it apply?
- What should travellers do?
- Which official source confirms it?

Suggested structure:
1. Direct, factual opening.
2. Name the country, route, airline or traveller group affected.
3. Explain the practical impact.
4. Give the next action.
5. Show or name the official source.

Goal: Useful completion, saves, shares and trust—not alarm.

### Simo Traveller

Build videos around a clear travel desire or decision:
- Is this destination worth visiting?
- How do you get there?
- What does a trip cost?
- What should a first-time visitor know?
- What can a traveller see or do?

Suggested structure:
1. Visual or spoken hook.
2. Show the destination or experience.
3. Add useful context: route, timing, costs or practical tips.
4. Deliver the promised answer.
5. Invite a relevant question or follow.

Goal: Storytelling combined with practical value.

## 7. Testing Framework

For each test, record:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | What do we expect to improve? |
| Brand | Travel Alerts.ke or Simo Traveller |
| Topic | The video subject |
| Variable | Hook, length, pacing, format, caption or CTA |
| Baseline | Comparable recent videos |
| Observation period | Consistent period after publishing |
| Results | Views, watch time, completion, shares, saves, comments and follows |
| Decision | Keep, adapt, retest or stop |

Change one major variable at a time where possible.

One result is not proof of a universal rule.

## 8. Common Myths to Avoid

Do not present these as established facts:
- Every video is always tested on a fixed-size first audience.
- There is a guaranteed ideal video length.
- Posting at one exact time guarantees reach.
- A particular number of hashtags guarantees distribution.
- Using a trending sound guarantees recommendation.
- A new account is automatically suppressed.
- A low-view video proves the account is shadowbanned.
- Deleting and reposting automatically improves reach.
- Follower count alone determines For You distribution.

Treat these as hypotheses only when account data justifies testing them.
