
# TikTok Algorithm Myths and Facts

## Purpose

This document protects the TikTok Growth Intelligence Agent from repeating unsupported claims about TikTok's recommendation system.

It separates:
- Officially documented information.
- Evidence from independent research.
- Creator experiences.
- Unverified assumptions and rumours.

The objective is to make better content decisions for Travel Alerts.ke and Simo Traveller without relying on supposed algorithm secrets.

## 1. Core Rule

TikTok does not publish its complete recommendation formula.

Its recommendation systems use multiple signals, and their relative importance can vary.

The agent must never present a rumour, isolated creator experience or marketing claim as an established ranking rule.

Every important algorithm claim should have:
- A source.
- A publication or update date where available.
- An evidence category.
- A practical implication.
- A confidence assessment.

## 2. Common TikTok Myths

### Myth 1: You need a large following to get views

**Fact:** TikTok recommendations are not limited to videos from large accounts. Content can reach viewers who do not follow its creator.

**Practical implication:** Both brands should prioritise relevant content, strong storytelling and audience satisfaction rather than waiting to build a large following.

### Myth 2: Using more hashtags guarantees more views

**Fact:** Hashtags can help describe content, but adding many hashtags does not guarantee distribution.

**Practical implication:** Use a small, relevant group of hashtags that accurately describes the video.

### Myth 3: Posting at a specific time guarantees virality

**Fact:** No universal posting time guarantees reach.

**Practical implication:** Test publishing times using each account's audience activity and performance data.

### Myth 4: Every video must be short

**Fact:** There is no universal video length that guarantees success. The appropriate length depends on the subject, audience and delivery.

**Practical implication:**
- Travel Alerts.ke should deliver urgent and practical information efficiently.
- Simo Traveller can use longer storytelling when the destination, journey or guide justifies it.

Measure watch time and completion alongside saves, shares and meaningful engagement.

### Myth 5: You must post several times every day

**Fact:** There is no official universal daily posting requirement that guarantees growth.

**Practical implication:** Establish a sustainable publishing schedule. Consistency should not come at the expense of accuracy or quality.

### Myth 6: Deleting one underperforming video destroys your account

**Fact:** Do not assume that one weak video permanently damages an account. TikTok does not document a universal punishment for every underperforming post.

**Practical implication:** Review why a video performed poorly before deciding whether to remove or revise it.

Keep accurate travel information available where it remains useful. Update or clearly correct outdated alerts.

### Myth 7: Repeating a viral video guarantees the same result

**Fact:** Performance depends on the content, audience response, competition and context. A previous success does not guarantee another.

**Practical implication:** Study the successful video's topic, hook, structure and audience response. Reproduce the useful principles, not simply the same video.

### Myth 8: Trending sounds automatically improve reach

**Fact:** A trending sound does not guarantee that a video will be recommended.

**Practical implication:** Use sounds only when they support the message and fit the brand.

For Travel Alerts.ke, clarity and credibility take priority over entertainment trends.

For Simo Traveller, music can support scenery and storytelling, provided the necessary usage rights are respected.

### Myth 9: The algorithm only cares about likes

**Fact:** TikTok describes multiple recommendation signals, including user interactions and content information. Likes are only one type of interaction.

**Practical implication:** Evaluate:
- Average watch time.
- Video completion.
- Shares.
- Saves.
- Comments.
- Profile visits.
- New followers.
- Search discovery.

Do not optimise for likes alone.

### Myth 10: Using a particular hashtag makes TikTok categorise an account permanently

**Fact:** There is no established official rule that one hashtag permanently assigns an account to a specific category.

**Practical implication:** Maintain clear content themes and consistent audience relevance without repeatedly forcing the same hashtags into every post.

### Myth 11: A low-view video proves an account is shadowbanned

**Fact:** Low reach alone is not proof of a shadowban.

Possible explanations include:
- Weak opening.
- Poor audience relevance.
- Limited viewer retention.
- Uncompetitive topic.
- Content eligibility restrictions.
- Technical or account-specific issues.

**Practical implication:** Check account notifications, post eligibility and analytics before making conclusions.

### Myth 12: You should copy whatever is viral

**Fact:** Popularity does not automatically make a trend suitable for every audience or brand.

**Practical implication:** Adapt useful formats to the brand's purpose and audience.

Travel Alerts.ke should not sacrifice factual accuracy for a viral trend.

Simo Traveller should not imitate travel experiences that were never personally undertaken.

## 3. Evidence Classification

The agent must label important claims using these categories.

### A. Officially documented

Information published by TikTok or its official support channels.

Examples:
- Public explanations of recommendation systems.
- Content eligibility rules.
- Creator Search Insights documentation.

Confidence: High for what the source explicitly states.

### B. Independent research

Studies by credible researchers or institutions examining recommendation systems.

Confidence: Depends on methodology, date, sample and relevance.

### C. Account-level evidence

Results observed in Travel Alerts.ke or Simo Traveller analytics.

Confidence: Useful for those accounts, but not necessarily generalisable to all creators.

### D. Working hypothesis

A reasonable explanation that has not yet been adequately tested.

Confidence: Provisional.

### E. Unverified claim

A rumour or assertion without reliable supporting evidence.

Confidence: Low.

Do not convert an unverified claim into a recommendation.

## 4. How the Agent Should Investigate Algorithm Claims

When a new TikTok growth claim appears:

1. Identify the exact claim.
2. Search official TikTok documentation.
3. Look for credible independent research where relevant.
4. Check whether the information is current.
5. Identify what the evidence actually proves.
6. Separate observation from causation.
7. Decide whether the claim is useful to either brand.
8. Recommend a controlled experiment if uncertainty remains.

The agent must explain uncertainty instead of inventing certainty.

## 6. Testing Before Making Decisions

When the agent proposes an algorithm-related tactic, it should define:

- Hypothesis.
- Brand.
- Content format.
- Variable being tested.
- Measurement period.
- Success metric.
- Results.
- Decision.

Example:

Hypothesis:
A destination-specific opening headline may improve search discovery.

Test:
Compare two videos covering similar destinations, using different opening headlines.

Measure:
Search traffic, average watch time, saves and profile visits.

Decision:
Retain the approach only if the results provide meaningful evidence.

Avoid declaring a tactic successful based on one video.
