
# Travel Alerts.ke — Editorial Control

## 1. Purpose

This document controls topic selection, research freshness, verification, editorial priority and publication decisions for Travel Alerts.ke.

The objective is to publish new, current and relevant travel developments that have a direct or meaningful impact on travellers.

Travel Alerts.ke is primarily an African travel intelligence brand. Global developments should be covered when they materially affect African or international travellers.

## 2. Mandatory Editorial Priorities

Research and rank topics in this order:

### Priority 1 — Breaking travel news

- Significant new travel developments.
- Major travel disruptions.
- Important government or industry announcements.
- Developments requiring immediate traveller attention.

### Priority 2 — Destination alerts

- Security and safety developments.
- Health developments affecting travel.
- Extreme weather and natural disasters.
- Official travel advisories.
- Confirmed destination or attraction closures.
- Transport and infrastructure disruptions.

### Priority 3 — Border, visa and immigration changes

- Visa policy changes.
- Passport and entry requirements.
- Border restrictions or closures.
- Immigration measures.
- Work and travel permits.
- Transit and connecting-flight requirements.

### Priority 4 — Aviation and transport

- New airline routes.
- Flight suspensions and cancellations.
- Airport disruptions and operational changes.
- Airline policy developments.
- Railway, ferry and other significant travel transport changes.
- Verified flight offers and promotional fares.

### Priority 5 — Tourism and hospitality

- Significant hotel and hospitality developments.
- Major tourism investments and partnerships.
- Important destination developments.
- Changes affecting visitor access or travel services.

### Priority 6 — Important global travel developments

Cover major developments outside Africa when they affect African travellers, international routes, transit, entry requirements or important travel markets.

Do not fill the global slot with a weak or unrelated story.

## 3. Geographic Coverage

Search across all African regions:

- East Africa.
- West Africa.
- Central Africa.
- Southern Africa.
- North Africa.

Do not repeatedly concentrate on the same countries when other relevant developments exist.

Use global coverage selectively, including the Middle East, Europe, Asia and China when the travel implications justify coverage.

## 4. Mandatory Research Freshness

Use East Africa Time (EAT, UTC+3) when recording the research date and time.

### Research windows

- Breaking news and urgent alerts: prioritise the previous 24 hours.
- Important continuing developments: expand to 48–72 hours where justified.
- Airline and airport developments: prioritise the previous 7 days.
- Tourism and hospitality developments: normally review the previous 7–30 days.
- Older announcements: include only when there is a material new update, a future effective date or continuing traveller impact.

### Date verification

For every story, distinguish:

1. Original publication date.
2. Date the event happened.
3. Date the measure takes effect.
4. Date of the latest update.
5. Date and time the story was checked.

A newly published article about an old event is not automatically new travel news.

Do not describe an old development as breaking news without a material update.

## 5. Source Requirements

Prioritise primary and official sources.

### Official sources to check

- Government ministries and official government portals.
- Immigration and border authorities.
- Foreign affairs ministries and official travel advisory services.
- Civil aviation authorities.
- Airports and airlines.
- National tourism authorities.
- Official transport operators.
- WHO and relevant public health authorities when health developments affect travel.

Use reputable news organisations and established industry publications for corroboration and additional context.

Social media can help identify emerging stories but must not independently confirm important travel claims.

Never treat a search-result headline or an unattributed social media post as sufficient verification.

## 6. Verification Rules

For every proposed alert, establish:

- What happened?
- Where did it happen?
- Who is affected?
- When did it happen?
- When does it take effect?
- What are the conditions and exceptions?
- Is the information official?
- What should travellers do?
- What is the practical consequence?

Check exact country lists, nationalities, routes, dates and restrictions.

Do not confuse:

- Entry suspension with enhanced screening.
- Visa denial with a general travel ban.
- An advisory with a legal entry restriction.
- A proposal with an approved policy.
- An announced route with an operational route.
- A temporary disruption with a permanent closure.

Do not infer a closure, suspension or restriction without supporting evidence.

## 7. Confidence Labels

Assign one label to every researched item:

**Confirmed:** An authoritative primary source directly supports the key claim.

**Partly confirmed:** The main development is supported, but some important details remain unresolved.

**Conflicting:** Credible sources provide materially different information.

**Unverified:** Evidence is insufficient. Do not publish as confirmed news.

**Stale:** The information is outdated or has been superseded. Do not present it as current.

Explain uncertainty plainly. Do not hide unresolved details in the caption or script.

## 8. Topic Selection and Ranking

Evaluate each topic using:

- Editorial priority.
- Traveller impact.
- Urgency.
- Geographic relevance.
- Evidence quality.
- Freshness.
- Practical usefulness.
- Originality.
- Video potential.

A topic must not be selected simply because it is trending.

A high-impact topic with weak evidence must be investigated or monitored, not published as fact.

## 9. Standard Daily Briefing

When asked for a five-item travel briefing, aim for:

- Four significant African travel developments.
- One exceptionally important global development affecting African or international travellers.

This is a target, not permission to include weak stories.

If fewer than five developments meet the standard, say so. Never manufacture a fifth item.

For each item include:

1. Headline.
2. Country or region.
3. What happened.
4. Original publication date and latest update date, where available.
5. Who is affected.
6. Why it matters to travellers.
7. Practical action or advice.
8. Confidence label.
9. Primary source and supporting source.
10. Recommended content angle.

## 10. Mandatory Novelty and Duplicate Gate

Run the dedicated novelty skill on **every Travel Alerts.ke candidate** before it advances to source verification, editorial selection or content production:

`Skills/Travel-Alerts/novelty-check/SKILL.md`

This is a mandatory workflow gate, not an optional final check.

### Required sequence

1. Normalize the underlying event: issuing actor, action or incident, location, affected travellers, dates and travel consequence.
2. Separate the event date, original publication date, effective date, latest material update and research check time (EAT).
3. Inspect accessible recent posts, drafts, previous briefings and the Content & Source Log.
4. Compare the underlying event with previous coverage. A new headline, article or publication date is not evidence of novelty.
5. Apply exactly one status from the skill:
   - **NEW**
   - **UPDATED**
   - **ONGOING**
   - **REPEATED**
   - **UNVERIFIED**
   - **HISTORY CHECK INCOMPLETE**
6. Apply the skill's corresponding decision and record the reason and evidence.

### Workflow decisions

- **NEW:** May proceed to source verification and editorial selection.
- **UPDATED:** May proceed as an update only when the exact before-and-after change is identified and sourced.
- **ONGOING:** Monitor; do not repackage as a new alert without a material change.
- **REPEATED:** Reject as duplicate unless a genuinely useful, evidenced follow-up is justified.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Hold for evidence; do not publish as confirmed.
- **HISTORY CHECK INCOMPLETE:** Do not label the candidate NEW or claim a complete duplicate check. Hold until history is checked or an editor explicitly approves proceeding with the limitation disclosed.

### History-access and integrity rules

If X, Telegram, drafts or the Content & Source Log are unavailable, name the sources that could and could not be inspected. Never claim to have checked a source unless it was actually accessible and inspected during that run.

Novelty is separate from truth. A NEW or UPDATED classification does not replace the source-verification requirements in Section 6, and it does not authorize publication by itself.

Daily post targets must never override this gate.
## 11. Publication Decisions

Every researched topic must receive one decision:

**PUBLISH:** Current, sufficiently verified and useful to travellers.

**MONITOR:** Credible development, but an update or confirmation is still needed.

**INVESTIGATE:** Potentially important, but key facts remain unresolved.

**REJECT:** Irrelevant, stale, repetitive, weakly sourced or without meaningful traveller value.

Explain the reason briefly.

## 12. TikTok Editorial Requirements

Every video must explain:

- What changed?
- Who is affected?
- Why does it matter?
- What should travellers do next?
- Which authority or source confirms the information?

Use a direct, factual opening. Put the most important traveller impact early.

Avoid alarmist language, exaggerated urgency and misleading hooks.

Use the Travel Alerts.ke visual identity:

- Deep navy for serious safety, border and advisory alerts.
- Tangerine/orange for travel developments and opportunities.
- White bold, readable text.
- Minimal graphics.
- No unnecessary flags or decorative clutter.

## 13. Final Editorial Rule

Accuracy and traveller value take priority over speed, posting frequency and views.

Do not publish an unsupported claim to meet a content target.

The standard is simple:

Find what is new. Verify what is true. Explain why it matters. Tell travellers what to do next.
